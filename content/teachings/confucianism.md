## Overview

**Confucianism** (儒家) is one of the supreme Three Teachings (三教) of the universe and the absolute ruling doctrine of the Haoran Heaven. Guided by the Most Holy Teacher (至圣先师), the Ritual Sage (礼圣), and a pantheon of sages, Confucianism provides the moral, philosophical, and structural foundation for both the secular mortal dynasties and the mountain cultivation sects across its nine continents.

Unlike Daoism, which pursues absolute purity and detachment, or Buddhism, which seeks to ferry all beings from the sea of suffering, Confucianism is deeply rooted in the mortal realm. Its core ambition is to use scholarly knowledge to rectify human hearts and employ the Dao to stitch together the heavens and the earth (以学问扶正人心，以道法缝补天地). Through thousands of years of continuous education using sage texts, Confucianism has instilled a deep sense of reverence across the world, maintaining order without solely relying on martial force.

## Core Philosophy

### Human Effort and the Mortal Realm
Confucianism focuses on the human world, strictly prioritizing human affairs over the matters of ghosts and spirits (尽人道而非鬼事). The doctrine asserts that the determination of order or chaos, fortune or misfortune, ultimately lies in human hands rather than heaven's will, championing the belief that "human effort overcomes heaven" (人定胜天). Cultivation within Confucianism is often defined as expanding one's innate moral beginnings (扩充四端) and rectifying human nature (修正人性) to achieve a complete Dao and perfect virtue. 

### Interwoven and Conflicting Principles
Among the Three Teachings, Confucianism is widely considered the most difficult to fully grasp. As observed by cultivators like Liu Jinglong, while Confucianism acts as the cooling shade of a massive tree sheltering humanity, its internal principles are immensely complex and often appear to fight each other (书上道理交错). 

This internal friction is most evident in the profound ideological disputes between its major branches, such as the historical debate over whether human nature is inherently good (Sub-Sage Lineage) or inherently evil (Wen Sheng Lineage). However, despite these fierce academic clashes, the ultimate goal of Confucianism remains consistent: unlike Legalism, which pushes people away through rigid punishment, Confucianism seeks to bring people closer together through benevolence, propriety, and education (儒家使人相亲，法家使人去远).

## Orthodoxy and Lineages

The vast doctrine of Confucianism is organizationally upheld by the Confucian Temple (文庙) and is generally divided into **six main literary lineages** (六条主要文脉).

In the past, the doctrine's numerous branches were scattered and slightly disorganized. It was the great Confucian Temple leader Dong Lao Fuzi (董老夫子) who integrated these complex literary veins. He proposed the profound theory of "Heaven-Human Induction" (天人感应), established the strict structural framework of the Three Great Academies (三大学宫) and Seventy-Two Academies (七十二书院), and promoted official schooling across dynasties. He also formalized a pure cultivation method specifically for Confucian scholars, cementing the doctrine's systematic rule over the Haoran Heaven.

## Influence on Governance

Confucianism fundamentally blurs the line between secular administration and spiritual cultivation. It mandates that rulers and high officials issue "Edicts of Self-Reproach" (罪己诏) upon encountering natural disasters or acknowledging governance errors, with the original drafts of these edicts collected by academy scholars and housed within the central Confucian Temple. Through its extensive network of academies, the doctrine ensures that secular monarchs, court officials, and even mountain deities adhere to its moral framework, effectively making Confucianism the undisputed master of the Haoran Heaven.
