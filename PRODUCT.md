# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Fans of Jian Lai (剑来) — Chinese donghua and webnovel readers, lore researchers, wiki browsers. They arrive from search, fan communities, or direct links. Their context is deep reading: cross-referencing characters, tracing cultivation paths, following timelines, understanding faction politics. They may be native Chinese readers, bilingual, or English-only. Many are re-readers seeking structured reference over narrative retelling.

## Product Purpose

A premium fandom encyclopedia that organizes the vast lore of Jian Lai into a structured, browsable archive. The wiki exists because the novel's world is enormous — hundreds of characters, dozens of factions, multiple cultivation systems, a sprawling timeline — and no structured English-language reference existed before this.

Success looks like: a reader can find any lore element within two clicks, understand its relationships to other elements, and trust the encyclopedic accuracy (cited, verified, sourced). The archive should feel like a physical sword manual or scholarly folio — not a generic wiki, not a fandom Fandom.com clone, not a SaaS dashboard.

## Brand Personality

**Scholarly. Cinematic. Reverent.**

- **Scholarly** — Encyclopedic precision. Cited sources, structured entries, bilingual labels (Chinese + English). The archive respects the reader's intelligence.
- **Cinematic** — The novel is epic; the wiki should feel epic. Motion, imagery, and pacing evoke the donghua's visual language — ink-wash atmospheres, sword-slash transitions, mist and cinnabar.
- **Reverent** — This is a labor of love for a beloved work. Every design choice honors the source material. No ironic distance, no casual meme energy, no "just a wiki" minimalism.

Voice: formal but warm, precise but not dry, literary but accessible. Never corporate, never casual, never ironic.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS dashboards** — No app-shell layouts, no sidebar nav, no data tables as default presentation.
- **Fandom.com / wiki farms** — No cluttered ad-driven layouts, no default MediaWiki skins, no "community portal" energy.
- **Mobile game UI** — No neon borders, no glowing skill trees, no gacha-style card reveals.
- **Purple-blue AI gradients** — No generic "tech" gradient backgrounds, no glassmorphism, no aurora effects.
- **Glossy card grids** — No identical rounded cards with icon + heading + text repeated endlessly.
- **Anime attack effects** — No energy beams, no speed lines, no particle explosions as decorative elements.
- **Over-animation** — Motion serves meaning. No animation for animation's sake, no bounce/elastic easing, no parallax that fights the reader.
- **Canvas/WebGL replacing content** — The wiki is text-first. Visual effects enhance; they never replace readable prose.

## Design Principles

1. **Archive as artifact.** Each page is a folio leaf, not a web screen. The design should evoke a physical sword manual — parchment, ink, seal stamps — without literally simulating paper textures.
2. **Sections are chapters.** Each major content area (Characters, World, Swordsmanship, etc.) has its own visual identity — its own accent color, mood, and spatial rhythm — while sharing structural DNA. A reader should feel they've entered a new chapter, not a new website.
3. **Cinematic pacing, scholarly restraint.** Hero sections and transitions can be bold and atmospheric. Body content must be calm, readable, and well-spaced. The shift from cinematic to scholarly is deliberate, not a compromise.
4. **Bilingual by design.** Chinese characters and English text coexist by structural choice, not as an afterthought. Chinese display text uses the calligraphy accent font; English uses the serif/sans system. Neither language is secondary.
5. **Earned motion.** Every animation has a semantic reason. Reveals mark content hierarchy. Transitions signal navigation. Staggered entrances group related items. If removing an animation doesn't lose meaning, it shouldn't exist.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

**Target: WCAG AA**, with the following specific commitments:

- **Color blindness aware.** Never rely solely on color to convey meaning. Status badges, category tags, and importance indicators must include text labels, icons, or patterns alongside color. The section-theme system uses distinct accent hues; ensure they remain distinguishable under deuteranopia, protanopia, and tritanopia simulations.
- **Screen reader structure.** Semantic HTML landmarks (<header>, <nav>, <main>, <footer>), proper heading hierarchy (no skipped levels), ARIA labels on interactive elements (search modal, dropdown menus, mobile nav toggle), and descriptive alt text on all imagery.
- **Keyboard navigation.** All interactive elements reachable via Tab/Shift+Tab. Visible focus indicators (not just browser defaults). Escape closes modals and dropdowns. Search accessible via / and Cmd/Ctrl+K.
- **Reduced motion.** Every animation wrapped in @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) with instant-transition or crossfade alternatives. No motion is decorative-only; all motion can be removed without losing content or meaning.
- **Contrast.** Body text ≥ 4.5:1 against its background. Large text (≥ 18px or bold ≥ 14px) ≥ 3:1. Placeholder text meets the same thresholds as body text.
